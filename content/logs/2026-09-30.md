#### B-Tree

##### Insert keys in random order vs Insert keys in sorted order

* Inserting keys in arbitrary order causes nodes to overflow and split at unpredictable points.
* The B-tree can be built bottom-up. Leaf nodes are packed sequentially to 100% capacity.

```
===================================================================================================================
                        B+ TREE CONSTRUCTION SIMULATION (Max Node Capacity = 3 Keys)
                        Target Dataset: [10, 20, 40, 50, 70, 90]
===================================================================================================================
STRATEGY A: Random Inserts (70 -> 20 -> 90 -> 40 -> 10 -> 50)    | STRATEGY B: Sorted Bulk-Load (10, 20, 40, 50, 70, 90)
-----------------------------------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------
STEP 1: Insert 70, 20, 90                                        | STEP 1: Pack Leaf 1 sequentially to 100%
   [ 20 | 70 | 90 ]  (Leaf 1 FULL)                               |    [ 10 | 20 | 40 ]  (Leaf 1 FULL)
                                                                 |
STEP 2: Insert 40  ==>  Triggers Split (Promote 70)              | STEP 2: Pack Leaf 2 sequentially to 100%
             [ 70 ]                                              |    [ 10 | 20 | 40 ]   [ 50 | 70 | 90 ]  (Leaf 2 FULL)
            /      \                                             |
   [ 20 | 40 ]    [ 70 | 90 ]                                    |
                                                                 |
STEP 3: Insert 10                                                | STEP 3: Build Parent Layer Bottom-Up
             [ 70 ]                                              |             [ 50 ]
            /      \                                             |            /      \
   [ 10 | 20 | 40 ]  [ 70 | 90 ]  (Leaf 1 FULL)                  |   [ 10 | 20 | 40 ]  [ 50 | 70 | 90 ]
                                                                 |
STEP 4: Insert 50  ==>  Triggers Split (Promote 40)              |
                 [ 40 | 70 ]                                     |
               /      |      \                                   |
      [ 10 | 20 ] [ 40 | 50 ] [ 70 | 90 ]                        |
-----------------------------------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------
FINAL METRICS:                                                   | FINAL METRICS:
  * Total Leaves: 3                                              |   * Total Leaves: 2
  * Memory Allocated: 9 slots (3 nodes × 3)                      |   * Memory Allocated: 6 slots (2 nodes × 3)
  * Space Utilization: 66% (3 wasted empty slots)                |   * Space Utilization: 100% (0 wasted slots)
===================================================================================================================
```

#### BFS+Bitmask: LC-3568. Minimum Moves to Clean the Classroom

##### Key Takeaways

###### 1. Integer IDs & Bitmasking

* Mapping: Assign each item `(row, col)` a unique zero-indexed ID `[0, K-1]` via hash map.
* State Representation: Store the collected items as an integer bitmask (`mask |= 1 << id`).
* Why: Provides `O(1)` item pickup and target checks (`mask == (1 << K) - 1`), while saving memory compared to sets or tuples.

###### 2. Pruning via State Dominance

* State Key: Define the visited state as `(row, col, item_mask)`.
* Pruning Rule: Only re-enqueue a state if it arrives with strictly higher energy than previously seen (`energy_levels[state] < current_energy`).
* Why?
    * `new <= old` (Dominated): Reaching the same tile with identical items collected but equal/less energy gives zero future advantage.
    * `new > old` (Useful): Extra energy unlocks previously unreachable tiles (e.g., distant resets), justifying re-exploration.
