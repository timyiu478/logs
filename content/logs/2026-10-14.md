#### Bitwise: LC-260. Single Number III

* **Key Insight:** Taking the XOR of all elements gives us the XOR of the two unique numbers because duplicate numbers cancel out ($x \oplus x = 0$).
* **Separation Logic:** Any `1` bit in $a \oplus b$ means that bit is set in **either $a$ or $b$, but not both**. 
* **Implementation Trick:** Use `isolated_bit = x & -x` to grab the rightmost set bit, then partition the array into two groups based on whether `n & isolated_bit` is true or false. XORing each group separately isolates $a$ and $b$.

#### Two Pointer: LC-16. 3Sum Closest

* **Why it works:** Because the array is **sorted**, moving pointers has a predictable effect on the sum:
  * **`current_sum < target` (Sum is too small):** We need a larger sum to close the gap. Increment the left pointer (`l += 1`) since elements to the right are larger.
  * **`current_sum > target` (Sum is too large):** We need a smaller sum to close the gap. Decrement the right pointer (`r -= 1`) since elements to the left are smaller.
